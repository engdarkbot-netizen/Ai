## First-Half 2026 Review

**TASI ended 1H 2026 at 10,799.92**, up 3% year-to-date but down 3.26% versus mid-2025, weathering regional conflict, an April peak at 11,589.05, and summer liquidity drought.

**Market capitalization rose 3.40%** y-o-y to SAR 9.44 trillion (USD 2.52 trillion), boosted by bonus issues from Al Rajhi, Riyad Bank, and Alinma, plus fresh listings.

**Liquidity deteriorated sharply**: Value traded fell 10.39% y-o-y to SAR 616.57 billion (USD 164.42 billion) and number of trades dropped 11.79%. Only two IPOs occurred in 1H versus six in the prior-year period.

**Sector performance was bifurcated**: Insurance led (+23.59% YTD) followed by Energy (+9.84%), while Media & Entertainment cratered (-35.90%) and Healthcare (-11.89%) and Transportation (-11.26%) lagged.

**The trailing P/E ended 1H at 16.0x**, down slightly from 16.4x in January, with Americana accounting for 12.23% of all shares traded while Al Rajhi (SAR 47.5bn) and Aramco (SAR 43.6bn) led value traded.

## Regulatory & Market Structure

**Foreign ownership regime transformed**: Effective February 1, 2026, the CMA scrapped the Qualified Foreign Investor regime and swap framework, opening the main market to all categories of foreign investors for direct purchase.

**Details on raising foreign-ownership caps expected in 2H**: The CMA flagged this mechanism late last year, which analysts expect could pull passive, index-tracking money into Saudi equities.

## Analyst Outlook

**Sico Capital's Head of Research Chiro Ghosh** expects sector rotation in 2H, with first-half leaders moderating while laggards including healthcare, materials, and capital goods recover as cyclical and macro headwinds ease. Banking underperformed as rate-cut expectations faded; materials faced pressure from anticipated supply increases as regional risk eased. One hedge: energy could soften if durable regional peace weakens oil-price expectations.

**Argaam Investments' Youssef Al Youssef** sees structural catalysts in the mechanism to raise foreign-ownership caps and expects Q2 results—the first full quarter capturing regional conflict impact—to drive real positioning. He views the 10,800 level as firm support even after July's test of 11,000.
