**Cambridge Unveils Self-Improving AI That Evolves Its Own Tests**

Researchers at Cambridge's Machine Learning Systems Lab, led by PhD student Alex Iacob under Professor Nic Lane, introduced the "Red Queen Gödel Machine" (RQGM), a framework that co-evolves AI agents alongside their evaluators to break through self-improvement ceilings. The system achieved 1.78-1.86x higher acceptance rates on scientific paper writing tasks compared to prior self-improving agents, while co-evolved graders reached 9% higher ground-truth accuracy on Olympiad-level mathematical proofs. In coding tasks, the RQGM used 1.35x-1.72x fewer tokens than prior state-of-the-art approaches. A hybrid architecture using NVIDIA Nemotron 3 Ultra for search and ChatGPT-5.5 for guidance reduced search-token costs by approximately 13x in paper-reviewing tasks. The approach draws from evolutionary biology's Red Queen hypothesis and replaces static benchmarks with evolving evaluators that become more demanding as agents improve. The system corrected a significant bias where baseline reviewers over-accepted AI-generated papers at up to 1.91x the rate of human papers. The method will be open-sourced to support wider adoption.
