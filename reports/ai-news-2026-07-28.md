## Top Stories

**EU AI Act Enforcement Begins in 5 Days, Applies to Nearly Every System in Europe**

The European Union's AI Act becomes fully enforceable on August 2, 2026, marking what legal analysts describe as "the most consequential single date in AI regulation history." The law applies to any AI system built by any company anywhere in the world that operates in or serves the European market. Core obligations include transparency requirements, risk classification, and documentation standards, with heavier requirements for higher-risk applications. The European Commission published final transparency guidelines on July 20, and the Council approved final amendments on June 29. The timing creates complications for AI companies mid-pricing-war, now forced to route releases through a regulatory framework that didn't exist a year ago. **Anthropic's Amodei: We Want Testing, Not Bans on Open-Weight Models**

Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei clarified the company's position after a week of criticism, stating Anthropic has never called for banning open-weight AI models. Instead, he proposes mandatory safety testing for "sufficiently capable" models before release, alongside tightened chip export controls and crackdowns on industrial-scale model distillation. The proposal requires global agreement, including from China, which Amodei concedes "may be possible" but remains the largest unanswered question. The testing would exempt less capable models from startups and academia, though the definition of "sufficiently capable" remains undefined. **Microsoft Unveils Multi-Model Agentic Cybersecurity Stack with Custom AI Model**

Microsoft announced Project Perception, an AI-powered security service launching in public preview August 3 that uses multiple AI agents to find vulnerabilities, simulate attacks, detect threats, and develop remediations. The service includes Microsoft's new MAI-Cyber-1-Flash model, trained specifically for vulnerability research in complex codebases. When combined with GPT 5.4 in Microsoft's MDASH harness, the duo achieved a 96% completion score on the CyberGym benchmark—12 points above Anthropic's Mythos 5 and OpenAI's GPT 5.6-Sol—at nearly half the cost. MAI-Cyber-1-Flash handles 90% of typical cybersecurity tasks, with GPT 5.4 covering heavier reasoning requirements. The approach demonstrates that purpose-built harnesses and model selection matter more than always using frontier models. [csoonline.com](https://www.csoonline.com/article/4202080/microsoft-unveils-multi-model-agentic-cyber-stack-for-security-operations.html)

## Policy and Regulation

**China Threatens Retaliation Over Potential US AI Sanctions**

China's government has vowed to "take all necessary measures" if the US imposes new AI sanctions, claiming US AI companies are distilling Chinese models. The statement comes amid escalating tensions over AI export controls, with the US government having already restricted access to Anthropic's Mythos 5 and Fable 5 models for non-US nationals. The dispute centers on chip export controls and allegations of smuggling advanced chips into China, which US officials argue is necessary to maintain America's AI advantage. **Industry Splits on Open Weights: 50+ Companies Sign Letter, Major Holdouts Remain**

The "Open Weights and American AI Leadership" letter now has 50 signatories including OpenAI, Google, Microsoft, Nvidia, Meta, AMD, Cisco, Cloudflare, GitHub, and Ollama. Anthropic and Amazon remain notable holdouts. On Monday, Nvidia launched the Open Secure AI Alliance with 35+ founding members including Microsoft, IBM, Hugging Face, Palantir, Red Hat, CrowdStrike, and the Linux Foundation—again without Anthropic. Nvidia argues defenders need open frontier models for incident investigation, citing a recent Hugging Face case where closed tooling blocked forensic work.
